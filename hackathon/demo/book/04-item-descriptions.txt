Relics of the Hollow Coast: 30 item descriptions
Pixel Harbor · written by Maya Lin Studio for Ethan Brooks

1. Keeper's Brass Key: Opens any lantern house on the coast. Warm to the touch, even in winter.
2. Gull-Feather Quill: Writes in ink that glows faintly for an hour.
3. Tidecaller's Shell: Hold it to your ear and hear the tide one minute early.
4. Salt-Cured Map: Shows the coast as it was a hundred years ago. Some paths still exist.
5. Lantern Wick (Everlasting): Burns for a week on a thimble of oil.
6. Fisher's Lucky Hook: Bent, rusted, and never once lost a fish.
7. Driftwood Charm: Carved by a child, blessed by a whole village.
8. Fog Glass: Look through it and the fog thins, just a little.
9. Keeper's Logbook: Every entry ends with the same line: "All lights burning."
10. Sea-Glass Lens: Turns lantern light into a narrow beam.
11. Copper Bell: Rings by itself when a storm is a day away.
12. Mended Net: Patched by forty hands over forty years.
13. Bottle of Dusk: Dark, cool and humming. Do not open indoors.
14. Lighthouse Shard: A piece of the great lens of Hook Point.
15. Weathervane Fish: Points toward the nearest lost thing.
16. Oilskin Cloak: Rain slides off it like it never fell.
17. Pocket Compass (Stubborn): Points north, eventually.
18. Smoked Herring: Restores a little warmth. Smells like home to someone.
19. Rope of the Seven Knots: Each knot unties a different kind of trouble.
20. Starfish Brooch: Glows under moonlight; worn by the old keepers.
21. Cracked Spyglass: Shows things a little closer, and a little stranger.
22. Lamp Oil (Fine): Burns clean and bright. Merchants cut it with water.
23. Grandmother's Thimble: Fits Wren perfectly, which is odd.
24. Whistle of Returning: Every boat on the coast knows this note.
25. Tin of Honey Candies: Calms frightened children and nervous goats.
26. Chalk of Marking: Leaves a mark only lantern light can reveal.
27. Wreck Plank: Carved with names nobody remembers.
28. Kelp Rope Sandals: Ugly, comfortable, grip any wet rock.
29. Moth Jar: The moths always fly toward the next lantern.
30. The Last Wick: Nobody knows what it lights. Yet.
